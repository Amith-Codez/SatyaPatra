01 · The verdict

Pick SIH26261: AI-Powered Application Validation, Document Authentication and Regulatory Compliance Verification

The SIH 2026 portal lists 265 problem statements. As of 22 October 2026, 260 of them are closed. 182 software PSs closed on 5 October, and the late batch your friend heard about (25 PSs) closed on 20 October, except five. Only five PSs still accept ideas until 28 October 2026, and all five are AICTE software PSs. So the real choice is between those five.

SIH26261 wins on every criterion you gave:

Free to build

Every component is open source: OCR, forensics, local LLM, satellite building data, PDF signature checks. It runs on a laptop for the demo and on government cloud in production.

Data exists

The PS explicitly allows synthetic or anonymised data. Public EoA letters, the APH 2024-27 handbook, the DocTamper forgery dataset and Google Open Buildings are all free.

Room for real innovation

Most teams will build "OCR + ChatGPT + dashboard". We add verification at the issuing authority, satellite checks on claimed buildings, and memory across institutions.

Easy to explain

"Upload a fake fire NOC. Eight seconds later it's flagged red, the issuer says it never issued it, and here's the evidence." Judges get it in one look.

Precedent: in SIH 2024, team Kal Hona Code (K L University) won the Software Edition with an "AI Supported AICTE Approval Process Portal" (SIH1729). AICTE has rewarded this kind of work before. We go one level deeper: they automated the workflow, we verify the truth of what flows through it.

05 · The solution

SatyaPatra: five engines, one evidence report

One sentence for judges: SatyaPatra checks every approval document at the source in seconds, and every flag it raises comes with proof. Each engine maps to a requirement written in the PS.

flowchart LR
  A[Institution uploads application + documents] --> B[READ: classify, OCR, extract fields]
  B --> C[CHECK: APH 2024-27 rulebook, expiry, cross-document and past-record consistency]
  B --> D[DETECT: PDF forensics, edits, copy-move, fonts, AI-generation, duplicates across institutions]
  B --> E[CONFIRM: issuer trust registry, Tier 1 signature/QR/DigiLocker, Tier 2 issuer portal, Tier 3 issuer confirms]
  B --> F[SEE: satellite footprint vs claimed area, photo geotag/time, photo reuse, lab equipment count]
  C --> G[Evidence report: 6 statuses per document]
  D --> G
  E --> G
  F --> G
  G --> H[Officer dashboard: risk queue, alerts, decision]
  H --> I[Hash-chained audit log]
  G --> J[Institution self-check before submitting]
READ

Understand any document

  • Classifies the document type and picks the right checklist for the application category (EoA, change of name, intake increase…)
  • OCR for English and Indian languages, plus stamp and seal detection
  • A local open-source LLM turns text into structured fields; outputs are validated against a schema
CHECK

Rules officers can edit

  • APH 2024-27 requirements written as YAML rules, each tagged with its handbook clause
  • Missing, expired and incomplete documents; land, built-up area and intake checked against norms
  • Cross-document checks: same address, owner and dates everywhere; compared with last year's approved record
DETECT

Forensics that explain themselves

  • PDF structure: incremental saves, edits after signing, editing-software signatures, white patches laid over text, a different font on a key field
  • Image: error-level analysis finds pasted regions in scans; DocTamper (CVPR 2023) is the benchmark for the next, learned detector
  • Exact fingerprints catch the same scanned seal or lab photo reused across institutions. Seals are matched to the issuer's registered specimen with OpenCV ORB + RANSAC: in our tests genuine scans scored 288+ matching points and fakes 29 or fewer, a gap image hashes could not show
CONFIRM

Verify at the source

  • Tier 1: digital signatures validated to India's CCA root; DigiLocker-issued documents; QR codes accepted only from allow-listed official domains
  • Tier 2: lookup on the issuer's own portal by reference number
  • Tier 3: one-click confirm or deny request to the issuer's registered contact, never a contact printed on the document
SEE

Check the physical claims

  • Google Open Buildings footprints and 2.5D heights inside the campus boundary, compared with the claimed built-up area
  • Photo EXIF: GPS inside the campus? Taken this year? Matches the claimed lab?
  • Open-vocabulary object detection counts workstations in lab photos against the APH norm
DECIDE

Humans stay in charge

  • Six statuses taken straight from the PS: Verified Mismatch Expired Pending Source unavailable Manual review
  • Risk-ranked queue, alerts, SLA reminders
  • Every rule run, issuer response and officer action goes into a SHA-256 hash-chained audit log

Ten differentiators to say out loud

  1. Verify at the source. Forgers can beat a detector. They can't make the issuer confirm a NOC it never issued.
  2. Never trust the document's own QR or phone number (exactly how the DU fakes worked). Only registry-listed official domains and contacts count.
  3. Space-verified infrastructure: satellite building data checks claimed area for free.
  4. Memory across institutions: the same scanned seal, photo or faculty member reused anywhere gets flagged.
  5. An evidence card for every flag: the cropped region, the rule, the APH clause and the source response. Easy to defend in a legal challenge.
  6. Rules as configuration: when the APH changes next year, officers edit rules, not code. Rules are versioned by academic year.
  7. Six statuses that match the PS exactly, so the evaluator can tick each requirement.
  8. Fully on-prem and open source: data never leaves AICTE (DPDP Act 2023), and no vendor lock-in.
  9. Institution self-check: colleges get a readiness score before they submit, so there are fewer deficiency rounds. The tool helps both sides.
  10. Tamper-evident audit chain: nobody can quietly edit a decision later. No blockchain cost.

13 · Judge interrogation

The hardest questions, with answers

Practise these out loud. A confident short answer beats a long one.

Need and problem

AICTE already has a portal and scrutiny committees. Why is this needed?

The portal collects documents; it can't confirm them. In 2017 the Hindustan Times reported that one in three technical institutes was caught filing false data for AICTE approval. The Hyderabad forgeries were found only after a manual referral of 76 NOCs. We make that check the default for every document, in seconds, and officers spend their time on the risky ones.

Isn't this a rare problem? Most institutions are honest.

Exactly why we rank by risk. Honest institutions go green fast and get approved sooner. The rare bad case is the one that costs lives or crores, as the cases on our slide show.

Who are your users, and did you talk to any?

AICTE regional officers and scrutiny committees, and institutions on the submitting side. We interviewed our college's AICTE coordinator, who files the EoA every year. (Do this before the deadline and quote them.)

AI reliability

What if your AI flags a genuine document as forged? You'll harass honest institutions.

Flags are advisory, as the PS requires. A red status means "needs review", never "rejected". Every flag shows its evidence and confidence, and the institution can respond in the portal. We tune the red tier for precision and publish our false-positive rate per document type.

Forgers will learn to beat your detector.

Forensics is only the second layer. The first is confirmation at the source: a forger can't make HYDRAA's records contain a NOC number that was never issued, and can't fake a CCA-rooted digital signature. Memory across institutions also catches reuse that individual forgers can't see.

Why not just use ChatGPT?

Three reasons. Institution data can't go to a foreign cloud. An LLM can read a document but can't tell whether it's authentic. And regulators need decisions they can reproduce. We use a local open model only to extract fields; deterministic rules and source checks make the calls.

How do you detect AI-generated documents?

We look for generation and editing traces (missing camera noise, uniform rendering, metadata, font anomalies). But we don't depend on it: an AI-made NOC still fails the issuer lookup.

Scanned documents with stamps, handwriting, poor quality?

An image-quality score comes first. A low-quality scan triggers "please re-upload", never a forgery flag, so rural or small institutions aren't penalised for bad scanners. Handwritten fields go to manual review.

What's your accuracy?

Give your measured numbers per engine (extraction F1, precision and recall per forgery type) and explain the test sets. Never quote a number you didn't measure.

Verification and integration

Most issuers, like a municipal fire office, have no API. How do you verify?

Three tiers. Tier 1: cryptographic checks (digital signatures, DigiLocker, official QR codes). Tier 2: the issuer's own online portal by reference number, where one exists. Tier 3: a one-click confirm or deny request to the issuer's registered official contact. If nobody answers within the SLA, the status becomes "Source unavailable" and the case goes to manual review. That status is listed in the PS itself.

Who maintains the issuer registry?

AICTE regional offices, seeded from government directories. Each successful verification strengthens it. Contacts printed on submitted documents are never added.

How is this different from DigiLocker?

DigiLocker only holds documents issuers push into it. Most fire NOCs, lease deeds, completion certificates and affidavits aren't there. We use DigiLocker where available and cover the rest.

How does it plug into the AICTE portal?

As a sidecar REST API. The portal sends an application ID and documents; we return a validation report as JSON with a webhook for status changes. AICTE's workflow and login stay as they are.

What if the authority itself issued a genuine but wrong NOC, for example through a bribe?

Then the document is authentic, and we say so. But our reality checks still apply: use mismatches (the Delhi basement case), satellite footprint against claimed area, and photos against claims. Those flag the gap between paper and reality.

Satellite and infrastructure

Satellite resolution is too low, and buildings have several floors.

We use it only as a sanity check for gross mismatches, such as 12,000 m² claimed against 3,000 m² of footprint. The 2.5D dataset gives height estimates to approximate floors. It triggers a decision to inspect; it never replaces inspection.

What about a new building that isn't in satellite data yet?

We check the imagery date. If the construction date is after it, the status is "pending evidence", and we ask for a geotagged construction photo set.

Privacy, law and ethics

Privacy and the DPDP Act?

Everything runs on-prem. Role-based access, encryption at rest, minimal retention. Personal IDs used for duplicate checks are salted hashes, never stored raw. Every access is logged.

Is an AI flag legally valid?

No, and we never claim it is. It's a preliminary indicator. The evidence is the issuer's response and the original document, preserved with hashes in the audit chain, which keeps the chain of custody intact.

Could this be misused to target certain institutions?

The same rules run on every application, rules are versioned and visible, and every officer action is logged in the hash chain. That makes selective targeting easier to detect than today.

Cost, scale and sustainability

What does it cost?

₹0 in licences, because every component is open source. It runs on CPU on existing government cloud. Compare that with commercial verification APIs charged per call, plus data leaving government servers.

Can it handle peak season when thousands of institutions upload at once?

Queue-based workers scale horizontally. Issuer lookups are cached, and heavy forensics run in batches. Each application targets under a minute on CPU, and we can show the benchmark.

The APH changes every cycle. Then what?

Rules are YAML, edited in a UI, versioned by academic year and tagged with clause numbers. No code change is needed.

What happens after the hackathon?

Pilot with our own college's EoA, then one regional office. It's open source under AICTE. The same engine fits UGC, NMC, NCTE, PCI and state boards. We'll apply for MIC implementation support.

Why not blockchain?

A hash-chained log gives tamper evidence without the cost or complexity. If needed, we can publish the daily root hash to a public ledger.

Team and execution

What have you actually built versus planned?

Be exact: show what runs, and list the rest as roadmap. Judges punish over-claiming more than unfinished work.

Why this tech choice (FastAPI, PostgreSQL, RapidOCR…)?

Have one line ready for every choice. For example: PostgreSQL because audit-grade records need transactions, and PostGIS adds campus polygons at scale; RapidOCR because it runs the PaddleOCR models on an ordinary CPU and is Apache-licensed.

Previous SIH winners built AICTE approval portals. What's new here?

They automated the workflow. We verify the truth of what goes through it: confirmation at the source, satellite checks, and memory across institutions. It complements a workflow portal rather than replacing it.
